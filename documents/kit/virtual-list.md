# Virtual list

**Scope:** The kit's virtual list: its default geometry, the text role it uses, and its surface and behavior.

The themes, the tokens and the rules every control shares are in [KIT.md](../KIT.md), whose [Controls](../KIT.md#controls) section says how this specification reads.

## Default geometry

Rows as their template draws them, each at its own height and 0.25rem apart, an options list's rows by `--nol-row-gap`, so a hovered option and a selected option beside it stand apart as two tabs' pills do; a row never measured takes its source's estimate, 120px unless the source gives one, and a measured row keeps its height while its content is unloaded; the rows within 600px beyond each edge of the view are rendered too, and rows 1,600px further are read, or as far as the owner asks, so the rows rendered do not grow with the list's length; options rows take tree-row geometry.

## Text role

Panel.

## Surface and behavior

The kit's list for long collections, as a named options list (`listbox`) or feed (`feed`), busy while rows load.
Options rows take the hover surface and the selected fill on the selected row; the owner's template draws a feed's articles.
Under forced colors the selected option is filled in `Highlight` with `HighlightText` text, and a [spinner](progress.md) in its template draws its arc in `HighlightText`, so it shows on the fill.
A feed's owner can hide a loaded row that its source must keep, with `nolVirtualRowHidden`, a function of the row's item: that row draws nothing and takes no space, so the rows around it stand one gap apart, it is no article and is hidden from assistive technology, and its place still counts in the positions of the rows after it; the reading position names the row after it, Tab passes over it, and a reveal of it shows the next row the feed draws, or the feed's end when none follows.
A feed is its own one Tab stop, and Tab goes on to the controls in its articles; with the keyboard focus it rings its view with a 1px accent outline inside its edges, or 2px `Highlight` under forced colors, a click into it shows no ring, and no article is ever ringed; `--nol-virtual-list-focus-width`, which once centered an article's ring, has no effect.
A row not yet loaded is blank space at its height until it loads.
A row whose image is still decoding keeps the height it had, which its template receives.
Once a read of rows in view has taken 250ms, a muted status line shows at the top when the first row in view is missing, and at the bottom otherwise, so a source that answers quickly never shows it; when a read fails, a field message there says "These items couldn't load.", with a secondary Retry button beside it.
What the person reads stays in place: rows measured, loaded, unloaded, added or removed above the first visible row, and a width change, move nothing in view, and each correction is made before the frame shows.
While a pointer drags the scrollbar's thumb, from its press in the scrollbar until it is released or cancelled or the window loses the focus, the list never sets the scroll position and keeps the scroll height it had when the press began, so the thumb follows the pointer at a steady size; the rows move with the thumb, each step a share of what is left toward that end, so the first row in view moves the thumb's way and both ends are reached, and rows measured, added or removed meanwhile move nothing in view, so rows that come at the end a feed follows wait beyond it, and the distance the feed keeps from that end leaves them out.
On release, one correction sets the true scroll position and height and moves nothing in view, except in a feed that follows its latest end: there it shows the rows that came at that end during the drag, at the distance from it the feed kept.
A wheel and the keys scroll as before.
While a person's wheel, key, pointer or touch scrolls the view, until that scroll ends, the list never sets the scroll position either, since a set ends a smooth scroll partway: a row measured at another height than its estimate moves the rows by the space before them instead, so nothing in view moves but by the scroll, and the scroll's end sets the position once, moving nothing in view. A change larger than the space before the rows is set at once.
An options list moves with the arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End, and Enter or Space chooses; a feed scrolls as a scrolling area does and moves no focus: Down and Up by 40px, Page Down and Page Up by seven eighths of its height, from anywhere in it unless a control handled them, Home and End to its start and end, the arrows, Home and End only while the feed or an article itself has focus, and Ctrl+Home and Ctrl+End leave it.
A feed follows its end, as a conversation does: it opens at its end, or at a saved reading position, a row and the distance into it, which the owner keeps, and each position it reports says whether it follows its end there, where the owner keeps none, and it reports one each time it starts or stops following, whatever made it, and opens at its end from a position that follows it; while it follows, a row changed anywhere, or added or removed at its end, brings the view to its true end, as a conversation does when a reply grows, on the first such change after any scroll of the person's own has ended, and rows that grow by themselves, or come or go at its start, such as older pages, keep the distance from the end a reader scrolled to within the last 120px.
A scroll position more than 120px from the end stops following; a scroll position the browser moves because a row shrank does not, nor one where the browser clamps the feed's scroll as its content shrinks, also when the clamp comes between two of the list's passes and its scroll event only after the content grew back. While a feed follows its end, a move toward its start that no wheel, key, pointer or touch on the view started is the browser's, also while a position the list set waits for its next pass, as one does after a row is added or grows at the end, and the feed goes back to the end at the distance it keeps; an app's script that sets the scroll with none of these before it is taken back too. A person's input counts for every frame of the scroll it starts, until that scroll ends. An input that scrolls nothing, such as a click or a keydown in a row, counts for the next scroll, whichever moves it. A list, a feed or not, rests where the browser clamps its own scroll, so rows added later at its end move nothing in view.
Coming back within 120px of the end, with the newest rows loaded, follows again.
A list takes its end rounded down to a whole pixel, for a feed to follow it and measure its distance from it, and for a key, a reveal or a part to stop at it: the browser rounds a scroll position to the nearer whole pixel, at any device pixel ratio, and one past an end that falls between two of them it takes back on a later layout, moving the view with nothing changed, so a list at such an end rests up to a pixel before it. A person's own scroll that the browser lets rest past such an end, at the browser's own end, is kept there, and a feed that follows keeps that distance from its end, so rows coming at its start, and rows measured at heights that still round to the browser's end, move nothing in view; once the heights round to less, so the browser's end no longer reaches the rest, the feed comes back to the list's end, as a row changed anywhere, or added or removed at its end, brings it there.
Jump to latest, by a pointer or a key, first tells the owner, so a source that holds only some of the rows can take the newest, and then scrolls to the end the source has then and follows it, never drawing the old rows' end. An owner that loads the newest rows later holds the jump: the feed keeps its view where it is, with Jump to latest hidden, until the owner gives it a new source; a reader who moves the view meanwhile ends the jump, and an owner whose load fails, or takes longer than it waits, releases it, so the feed scrolls to the end of the rows it has. A new source given as the owner is told, or the first one after it held the jump, opens at its end and follows it, though the reading position the owner gives still doesn't follow, and the feed reports that it follows; any later one opens at its position, as before.
A feed opened at a saved reading position that doesn't follow its end keeps that place as its rows measure, also where the rows' estimates put it past the end, and until the reader moves the view it follows only once the rows measured to the end, from the place's row or the view's, whichever lies farther from it, bring it within 120px of it.
An app's reveal puts a row at the view's start, middle or end, or scrolls the least that shows it, without moving the focus. It can name a part of the row instead, an offset into it or an element inside it, which the alignment then places. The list loads and renders a far row at once, and holds the row, or its part, where the reveal put it as that row and the rows around it are read and measured, as rows come or go before it, and, for an element, as the row grows or shrinks above it, until the reader moves the view; a row that the least scroll already shows is held where it is. An element outside the row reveals the whole row, and when the row is removed, the view rests at the start of the row that takes its place. A reveal that puts the view at a feed's newest end holds nothing: the view follows the newest rows from there.
An app's hold keeps an element inside a row where the view shows it now, as the row grows or shrinks above it, without moving the view.
An element taller than the view holds too.
The app calls it before it changes the row, since the hold reads the element's place from the current layout.
The hold lasts until the reader moves the view, as a reveal's does.
A feed that follows its newest end, or whose jump the owner holds, holds nothing, and an element outside the row changes nothing.
A click's keep of its row, and the stop it put on following, stay as they are.
A click on a resizing control keeps its row as it always does, and the element is then held from where the click put it.
A row in a feed whose resizing control the reader clicks, by pointer, Enter or Space, keeps its top where it is when that grows or shrinks it, following or not, and the rows after it move instead; any other click in a row, such as Copy, a link or a popup's trigger, holds nothing.
A resizing control is one marked `data-nol-resizes-row`, which the kit's controls that grow or shrink their own row carry: a tool call's chevron, Show all lines, Show more and its output's Retry; a code block's wrap toggle; a diff's layout and wrap toggles, file toggle, Load diff and hunk expanders; a message run's more; and a permission card's more.
An app marks its own controls that grow or shrink their row the same way, such as a step's expander; `aria-expanded` alone holds nothing, since a popup's trigger carries it too. The click counts until a measure changes that row, the reader moves the view by a scroll, a key, Jump to latest or a reveal, or a second passes, so a body that shows only after a fetch, or after other rows changed or rows came at the end, still keeps the row where it is then. When that puts the end out of view, the feed stops following, as a scroll away does, and follows again only once the end is back in view or the reader scrolls, not within 120px of it, so the row's later growth and rows added at the end move nothing in view; when it brings the end back, the feed follows again. A row that grows by itself, such as with streamed text, keeps the end in view while the feed follows.
A feed whose latest end is its start, for a newest-first list such as an activity log, does the same at its start: it opens at its first row, a row added at the start shows while the view is within 120px of the top and moves nothing in view otherwise, and rows added at its end, such as older pages, never move the view.
Changing which end is latest moves the view to the new end while the feed follows; while it doesn't, the feed follows again, without moving the view, when the view is already within 120px of the new end with the rows there loaded.
Scrolling never moves a row sideways.
